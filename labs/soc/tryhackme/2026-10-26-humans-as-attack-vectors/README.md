## Learning objective

Understand how attackers target people through social engineering, and how monitoring, reporting and security controls can help reduce the risk.

## Concepts I can now explain

- Social engineering can exploit trust, urgency or fear to influence a decision. People are one part of a wider security system, alongside processes and technology.
- A suspicious interaction provides context for investigation; it does not by itself prove that an account or device is compromised.
- User reports can be considered alongside email, identity and endpoint telemetry to assess what happened.
- Awareness training, accessible reporting channels and technical safeguards support one another.

## What was difficult

I initially found it difficult to understand why attackers might target people even when technical controls are present. My takeaway is that an attacker may try to influence a legitimate user into taking an unsafe action. The outcome depends on the situation and the safeguards in place; social engineering is not always easier than a technical attack.

## Interview explanation

Human-targeted attacks try to influence behaviour, for example by creating urgency or impersonating a trusted party. As a junior analyst, I would use the reported interaction and available telemetry to distinguish suspicious activity from confirmed compromise. I would document evidence, explain uncertainty and follow the appropriate escalation process.
